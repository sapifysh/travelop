Three days in Lombok: an itinerary that actually works.

Three days is enough to see the best of Lombok if you plan around the island's real distances. This route gives you one day for the south coast, one for the Gili Islands and one for the waterfalls of North Lombok, with no rushed mornings and no wasted hours in traffic.

Before you start: how to plan three days in Lombok

Lombok looks small on a map, but roads wind through hills and villages, so a drive that looks like 40 kilometres can take more than an hour. The secret to a good short trip is grouping sights by area instead of crossing the island every day.

Most travellers fly into Lombok International Airport (LOP) in Praya, Central Lombok. It sits close to the south coast, which makes the south a natural first day. The Gili Islands are reached from the northwest coast, and the big waterfalls are in Senaru, on the northern slopes of Mount Rinjani.

The best base for this route is Senggigi or the Mataram area on the west coast for nights two and three, and Kuta Mandalika for night one if you land early. If you prefer to stay in one hotel, choose Senggigi: it is roughly in the middle of all three days.

Day 1: South Lombok, Sasak villages and sunset at Bukit Merese

Start the morning in Sukarara, a village known for hand-woven Sasak songket. Women weave on traditional wooden looms, and you can see how a single piece of cloth takes weeks to finish. It is a good place to buy a textile directly from the people who made it.

Continue south to Sade, a traditional Sasak village near Rembitan where families still live in thatched houses with floors of clay and buffalo dung. A local guide usually walks visitors through the village and explains Sasak customs. A small donation or buying from the stalls is appreciated.

After lunch in Kuta Mandalika, spend the afternoon at Tanjung Aan, a wide bay with powdery sand and calm, clear water that is ideal for swimming. Finish the day by climbing Bukit Merese, the grassy headland next to Tanjung Aan. The walk takes about 15 minutes, and the view over the bays at sunset is one of the best on the island.

Day 2: The Gili Islands, turtles and a car-free island

Leave early for the northwest coast. Boats to Gili Trawangan, Gili Meno and Gili Air leave from Bangsal and Teluk Nare harbours, and the crossing is short. Public boats are cheap but wait until they fill up; a private boat leaves when you are ready and lets you visit more than one island.

Spend the morning snorkelling. Green sea turtles feed on the reefs around the three Gilis, and off Gili Meno you can swim over the underwater statues, a ring of life-size figures on the sea floor that has become a favourite with snorkellers and divers.

In the afternoon, stop on Gili Trawangan. There are no cars or motorbikes on the Gilis, so people get around by bicycle or horse cart. Rent a bike, ride the coastal path around the island and have lunch with your feet in the sand before heading back to Lombok.

Day 3: Tiu Kelep waterfall and the Senggigi coast road

On your last day, drive north to Senaru at the foot of Mount Rinjani, Indonesia's second-highest volcano. The drive is long, so leave after breakfast and break it up at viewpoints along the way.

Two waterfalls sit close together in Senaru. Sendang Gile is a short, easy walk on a paved path. Tiu Kelep takes longer: the trail crosses a river and ends at a wide curtain of water falling into a cool pool. Wear sandals or shoes that can get wet, and use a local guide if the river is high.

Return along the west coast in the late afternoon. The road between Pusuk and Senggigi follows the sea, and the Malimbu viewpoint is a lovely place to stop for sunset with Bali's Mount Agung on the horizon on a clear day.

Getting around: why a private driver makes three days easier

Public transport in Lombok does not reach most beaches, villages or waterfalls, and ride-hailing apps are limited outside the main towns. Renting a scooter is possible, but the distances in this itinerary, especially Day 3, are long for an inexperienced rider.

Practical tips for your Lombok trip

Best time to go: the dry season, roughly May to September, brings the clearest sea and the safest waterfall trails. The rainy season makes the island green and quiet, but some roads and trails can be slippery.

Dress respectfully: Lombok is a mostly Muslim island known as the Island of a Thousand Mosques. Cover your shoulders and knees when visiting villages and mosques, and keep swimwear for the beach.

Carry cash: ATMs are easy to find in Kuta, Senggigi, Mataram and Gili Trawangan, but small warungs, village stalls, parking and entrance fees usually take cash only.

Protect yourself from the sun: bring reef-safe sunscreen, a hat and water, especially for the Bukit Merese walk and a full day of snorkelling.

Frequently asked questions

Is 3 days enough for Lombok?

Three days is enough to see the south coast beaches, one day in the Gili Islands and the Senaru waterfalls if you group sights by area. With more time, add a slower beach day or a Mount Rinjani trek.

Where should I stay for a 3-day Lombok trip?

Stay in Kuta Mandalika for the south coast and Senggigi for the Gili Islands and the north. If you want one hotel for all three days, Senggigi is the most central base.

Can I visit the Gili Islands as a day trip from Lombok?

Yes. Boats leave from Bangsal and Teluk Nare on Lombok's northwest coast and the crossing is short. A private boat lets you snorkel with turtles, see the Gili Meno statues and stop on Gili Trawangan in one day.

How do I get around Lombok without a car?

Public transport rarely reaches beaches and waterfalls. Most visitors hire a private car with driver for the day, which is safer than a scooter on long routes and lets your group set its own schedule.

What is the best month to visit Lombok?

The dry season, roughly May to September, is best for clear water, snorkelling and waterfall trails. Shoulder months like April and October are quieter and still mostly dry.
